How to Use the Mining Material Finder

Start on the Locations & Percentages tab and choose a system — Stanton, Pyro, or Nyx — using the tabs above the location grid. Select up to three materials you need from the dropdown filters; the tool instantly narrows the list to locations that contain all of them and sorts the results by combined yield, so the best spot lands at the top. Each location card breaks results down by mining method (Ship, Vehicle — ROC and other ground vehicles — or Hand) so you know exactly what equipment to bring before you fly out. Prefer scanning in the field instead? Switch to the Signature Checker tab and enter the raw RS reading from your ship's HUD — the tool runs the cluster math instantly and tells you the material, rarity tier, and node count before you commit to a burn. The underlying data is rebuilt from the game's files for every major mining patch, including 4.10.

How to Read Ship, Vehicle, and Hand Mining Percentages

Every location card lists three potential mining methods — Ship, Vehicle (ROC and other ground vehicles), and Hand — and each material under those headings shows a percentage. That number is not how much of the material exists at the location; it's the statistical likelihood of encountering that specific resource when you scan or break open a node using that method at that location. A material listed at 35% under Ship Mining means roughly one in three ship-mineable deposits you crack open there will yield that resource, while the remaining nodes are made up of the other materials listed in that same group. Because the three methods pull from separate node pools, a location can have a high Quantainium chance via Ship mining and a completely different profile via Hand or vehicle mining. Always check the method that matches the equipment you're flying with — a location with great Ship percentages won't help you if you're running a ROC on the surface.

Best Locations for Hadanite, Quantainium, and Rare Ore in 4.10

If you're optimizing a mining loop for Star Citizen 4.10, start by matching the material to the method, because most of them only exist under one. Vehicle mining is the cleanest case: every Pyro body with a ground deposit table is a single material at 100% — Feynmaline on Ignis, Fuego, Terminus and Pyro IV, Glacosite on Monox, Adir and Vuur, Beradom on Pyro I, Bloom and Fairo — so a ROC or ATLS run there never wastes a scan. In Stanton the best ground deposit is Aberdeen, the moon orbiting Hurston, at 64% Feynmaline. Hadanite is hand-only and a flat 6% everywhere it appears, so pick the closest of the sixteen sites rather than hunting a better percentage. Quantainium is ship-only and never above 1.7%, and it decays from the moment you extract it, so route planning matters more than the number — filter for it above and find one close to a refinery. Rarer ores like Taranite, Bexalite, and Riccite tend to cluster in Pyro's less-traveled fields, rewarding pilots willing to fly further for a heavier payout per scoop. Use the material filters above rather than a static guide, since exact percentages and hot spots shift between patches.

System Breakdown: Stanton vs. Pyro

Stanton is the original, UEE-regulated system, and it shows in the mineral data: decades of corporate and player mining activity have thinned out the highest-purity nodes, so the average resource quality you'll encounter there skews lower, even though it's by far the safer system to operate in. Pyro is newer, lawless, and largely unmined, which means its asteroid fields and surface deposits still carry a noticeably higher concentration of high-tier materials. That's the basis of the Pro Tip above: if raw yield and material quality are your priority, Pyro consistently outperforms Stanton, at the cost of UEE security and longer, riskier supply runs. A handful of materials, including Sadaryx and Savrilium, are currently exclusive to Rock Breaker Contract locations regardless of system. Mining is also one of the most repeatable ways to grind aUEC, whether you're saving toward a bigger hull or just covering routine repairs and ammo — chaining high-percentage Ship and ROC locations through the filters above gets you to a usable total faster than mining wherever you happen to be.

Refining Methods: Speed, Cost, and Yield Trade-offs

Finding the ore is only half the loop — what you do with it at the refinery matters just as much. Star Citizen offers nine refining methods, split across three speed tiers (Slow, Normal, Fast) with three trade-off profiles in each: Efficient, Normal, and Wasteful. Efficient methods run slowest for their speed tier but return the highest yield per unit of raw ore, which is the right call for scarce or high-value hauls like Hadanite and Quantainium. Wasteful methods finish fastest for their tier but lose more material to slag, so save them for cheap, plentiful ore when turnover matters more than yield. Normal methods split the difference and are the sensible default when you're not optimizing for either extreme. Check the Refining Methods tab above to compare all nine side by side — speed, cost, and yield — before committing your haul to a refinery.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I use the RS signature scanner?

Switch to the Signature Checker tab and type the raw RS number shown on your ship's mining HUD into the scanner input. The tool runs exact cluster math against the full signature database and instantly returns the matching material, rarity tier, mining method, and how many nodes make up that cluster.

Where is the best place to mine Hadanite in Star Citizen?

Hadanite is hand-mined, not vehicle-mined, and it sits at a flat 6% wherever it appears — sixteen locations across Stanton and Pyro, including Aberdeen and Ita around Hurston, Cellin, Daymar and Yela around Crusader, and most of the Pyro bodies. Because the chance is the same everywhere, the best Hadanite spot is simply the closest one to you: filter for it above and pick by travel time, not by percentage.

Where do I find Quantainium in Patch 4.10?

Quantainium is ship-mined only — it appears in no vehicle or hand deposit — and it is rare wherever it shows up, peaking at 1.7% around Euterpe and Clio and never reaching 2% anywhere. Filter for it above to find the closest node, and plan the run before you scoop: it decays from the moment you extract it.

What is the fastest way to make aUEC for in-game ship prices?

There's no single fastest method, but chaining high-percentage Ship and ROC mining locations — especially for top-value nodes like Hadanite and Quantainium — is one of the most consistent, repeatable aUEC income loops in the game. Use the filters above to line up the best-yield locations for materials you can sell, then route to the trade terminal paying the most for your haul.

Where can I find Sadaryx and Savrilium?

Sadaryx really is exclusive to Rock Breaker Contract sites — hand mining at the Breaker Stations interior, at 25%. Savrilium is not: it is 66.7% at the Breaker Stations Large Geode, but it also turns up at 1.5% in both the Keeger Belt and the Glaciem Ring, so it can be ship-mined in Nyx without a contract. Check active Rock Breaker contracts first for either one — the odds there are far better.

Which Star Citizen refining method should I use?

It depends on what you're refining. Use an Efficient method — Dinyx Solventation, Ferron Exchange, or Pyrometric Chromalysis — for scarce or high-value ore like Hadanite and Quantainium, since it returns the highest yield per unit of raw input. Use a Wasteful method — Kazen Winnowing, Cormack Method, or XCR Reaction — for cheap, plentiful ore when you need fast turnover instead. Normal methods are a reasonable default when you're not optimizing for either extreme. See the Refining Methods tab above for the full breakdown of all nine methods.
